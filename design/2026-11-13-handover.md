## Lessons from the ROVS course (tell writers up front; the briefs cover most)

- The commonest cold-read fault by far: jargon and identifiers used before the module introduces
  them, or never introduced at all. A writer must introduce a term in the unit's own words, or cut it;
  never write a definition the unit doesn't give.
- A claim broader than the unit ("no topology gives one" where the unit says "none we tested").
- A count spoken that the slide doesn't show, and two figures for one event.
- The summary must say exactly "If you remember one thing, make it the sentence on the screen." The
  script check now refuses a module without it.
- Diagrams: a container box drawn after its inner boxes hides them, so list the container first. Four
  boxes in a 2 by 2 grid with arrows to one box below make the arrows cross the lower boxes; stack them
  in a column with the target to the right. A hand label between two boxes overlaps them unless the gap
  is wider than the text.
- The build voices every script in the course's video folder, approved or not. Approve a batch before
  building it, and merge the next batch's scripts only after that build has started.
