Unit 3 of Module 1

What it isn't: logical isolation and no physical air gap

By the end of this unit you'll be able to explain what "isolated" means for this vault, why it isn't a physical air gap, and what Northgate's account needs before it can create a vault.

About 10 minutesNo account needed5 steps

Northgate Freight is a made-up haulier. Its directors have heard the words "air gap". Before Northgate's IT manager uses those words, the manager needs to know exactly what the vault does.

The screen comes from the IBM Cloud console in our lab, and the results come from our tests.

Terms used. COS: short for IBM Cloud Object Storage. A COS instance is one copy of that service in an account. VSI: a virtual server instance, which is a virtual server in IBM Cloud. eu-de: the IBM Cloud region in Frankfurt. Deployable Architecture: a ready-made package in the IBM Cloud catalog that builds a set of cloud resources in your account. Audit events: records of who made which request to a service.

Step 1 · Explore the parts

What keeps the vault apart

The vault is isolated by network rules and by locks. It's still a bucket in a cloud region, and the networks on its allow-list reach it in the normal way.

What did the allowed network get from the vault in our tests?

Show the answer

Normal answers. A control request from the lab VSI inside the allowed VPC got HTTP 200. That's what logical isolation means here: the vault answers the networks on its list and refuses everyone else.

Whose account holds the vault's bucket?

Show the answer

An account that IBM manages. Our notes record that, and it fits what we saw: the bucket wasn't in our COS instance's bucket list, and the configuration API gave the owner 403.

The words on the page

Step 3 · Predict

Who gets in: networks and accounts

Two lists decide who gets in. The vault's own allow-list decides which networks can reach it. IBM keeps a second list that decides which accounts can create a vault. What do you think happens to a request from outside the allow-list, even with a valid key?

Which answer is right?

Show the answerCorrect. The allowed network got HTTP 200, and everyone else got HTTP 403.

The network rules are checked before the key counts, so a stolen key from outside the allow-list gets nothing.

Our account could list vaults, but at first every create failed. What did that tell us?

Show the answer

That a list works with a role alone, and only a create shows whether the account is on the list. Six creates went to failed in 17 to 40 seconds with no error text, and later a create went to active in about 30 seconds. IBM didn't confirm the cause, and the most likely one is that the account wasn't yet on the list. If your account is on Basic support you can't open the onboarding case yourself, so ask IBM's Cyber Vault product team, who allow-list the account when asked.

Step 4 · Spot the fault

What Northgate wants to tell the auditor

Northgate's IT manager wants to tell the auditor who checks Northgate's security controls: "The vault is physically air-gapped, and the owner can show who touched it." Which parts of that hold up?

What does the first part get wrong?

Show the answer

The isolation is logical. Network rules and locks keep the vault apart, and its allowed network can reach it.

The last part says the owner can show who touched the vault. What did our test show?

Show the answer

The owner got 403. The configuration calls returned HTTP 403 for the account owner. Of 4,346 events in the us-south logs instance, none came from Cloud Object Storage, and a scan of the eu-de instance found no vault event. IBM's overview calls audit logging "fully managed". So before you promise an auditor a record of who touched the vault, check that you can see the events from your own account.

Watch for this. Two more things to check in your own account. Allow time for a vault delete: in our lab one was accepted and still showed deleting at least six days later. After a failed create, look for keys left behind: in our lab one left two keys on the COS instance.

Step 5 · Check yourself

Three questions

Which phrase is right for this vault?

Show the answer

Logical isolation. The allowed network reached the vault and got HTTP 200, and everyone else was refused.

What does an account need before it can create a vault?

Show the answer

The allow-list as well as the roles. Listing vaults needs only a role. Only creating one shows whether IBM has allowed the account.

Is this vault the Deployable Architecture "COS Cyber Vault for Storage Defender"?

Show the answer

No. The vault is a managed COS feature. The Deployable Architecture is a separate product, and we never deployed it in our lab. Unit 1.4 puts them side by side.
